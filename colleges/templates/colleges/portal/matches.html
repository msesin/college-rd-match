The match % shows how well your grades, tests, interests and personality fit what each college looks for. It is not your chance of admission: even a 100% match can be turned down, especially when you ask for full financial aid. "UGS applicants" is how many UGS students have the college on their list: when many more UGS students apply to a college than it usually admits, the match goes down for all of them (by up to a fifth), so it changes as lists change. This page updates by itself. Your counselor will go through this with you.

Each match is built from ten factors. For each one, the "Why" column says whether you got full points, part of them, or none. How many points each factor is worth differs from college to college.

Where this comes from: almost every US college publishes a Common Data Set (CDS) each year: a standard report with its admissions numbers (applicants, admits, test scores, grades) and, in section C7, how much each factor matters in its decisions: very important, important, considered, or not considered. Nine of the ten factors below, and how much each college weighs them, come from the college's own CDS. Majors and UGS applicants are added by UGS.

SAT
Your SAT score against the scores of students the college admitted. At test-optional colleges you can apply without it; if your score is low for that college, we tell you not to send it.
Rigor of school record
How demanding your schooling is: the type of school (an English-language curriculum such as IB, AP or A-levels; a top Ukrainian lyceum or gymnasium; or a regular school) and your grades in the subjects of the major you want.
GPA
Your grades over the past two years, compared with the grades of the students the college admits.
Class rank
Where you stand in your class (top 1%, 5%, 10%, 25% or 50%), compared with the college's students. If your school does not rank, you get half of these points.
Character
How well your description of yourself fits the kind of student the college looks for.
Talent/ability
Awards, olympiads, research and other exceptional achievements (academic, artistic or athletic).
Extracurricular activities
How long, how deeply and with what leadership or impact you take part in activities outside class.
Volunteer work
Regular volunteering and community service.
Work experience
Paid work, a family business, or caring for family members.
Majors
How strong the college is in what you want to study, from program rankings (not the CDS). A college with nothing related to your majors is left out.
UGS applicants
Not in the CDS: when many more UGS students apply to a college than it usually admits, the match goes down a little for all of them.
